ShoutLine is an AI product that functions as your own personal media/news team. It started as an email digest product, but it is evolving quickly. It's in early access at [shoutline.co](https://shoutline.co).

<!-- essay -->

ShoutLine started as a tool I wanted for myself. I wanted more control over my relationship with the internet and the thousands of slop cannons aimed at me. That was a problem before AI, and it's still a problem now.

On the modern internet, an individual is up against extremely well-designed algorithms that can keep presenting interesting content indefinitely. For me, the main problem was Twitter. I was wasting precious hours constantly refreshing the feed.

But the difficulty is that there is useful information in there. Twitter, YouTube, and other platforms do surface interesting ideas, thoughts, and discoveries. I didn't want to lose access to that. I wanted to get the good stuff without being pulled into endless scrolling.

That's the reason I wanted an app that works against that dynamic. The problem isn't simply that some of the content is bad. It's the way you consume it: a feed that never ends and always offers the possibility that the next thing will be worth seeing.

And that's hard to compete with. I think the short-video algorithms behind YouTube Shorts and TikTok might be the most entertaining things humans have ever made. Minute for minute, on demand, they can be more entertaining than a video game, a livestream, or television. A close sporting final might beat them, but you can't reliably summon one whenever you want.

So the challenge is substantial. You're trying to change your relationship with something that's exceptionally good at keeping you entertained.

The first version of ShoutLine was a simple idea: take my feed, find the useful things, and send them to me periodically. Instead of opening an app and then reminding myself to get off it, I'd receive a notification saying, "We've found ten interesting things. Here they are."

The problem is that this is a utilitarian experience. It doesn't give you the same immediate reward as refreshing a feed. Even after building it, I found myself going back to Twitter. Delivering a useful digest doesn't automatically replace the habit.

ShoutLine uses AI agents to find, summarise, organise, and categorise information. You direct those agents towards topics you're interested in, using sources you provide. But a daily email is only one part of the idea. You could probably build that part yourself with a Claude or OpenAI subscription.

The larger problem goes beyond feeds. It applies whenever someone is telling you something and their interests aren't aligned with yours. That's the core problem I want ShoutLine to address. It's much harder than sending someone a daily summary of news about a topic they follow.

I've been describing ShoutLine as a personalised media company, or your own private news team. That captures something about how it works, but I'm starting to question whether it communicates why anyone should care.

People don't necessarily want a private news team. They want what having one could do for them.

If I reduce that to basic motivations, people want money, status, and to be more attractive to potential partners. Those are much more concrete desires than "having a news team". The product has to connect what it does to outcomes people actually want.

For example, you don't want to miss an emerging trend, an important piece of information, a useful new app, or an opportunity because your attention was scattered across too many things. A team working on your behalf should help you find those things and understand why they matter to you.

That suggests a more ambitious purpose for ShoutLine: [ShoutLine is an AI-powered media company personalised to you, whose job is to help you become wealthier, increase your status, and become more appealing to other people](https://shoutline.co).

That's my current thinking about why someone would buy it. The news team is the mechanism; the value comes from what it helps you do.

I think there's a gap between that and how the product is currently presented. Closing that gap should shape both how ShoutLine is sold and what we build next.
